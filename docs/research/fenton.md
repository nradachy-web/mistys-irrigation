# Fenton, MI: local research for irrigation copy

Researched 2026-11-10. Every fact below names the page it was read on. Copy on the Fenton
hub and the five Fenton service pages may use only what is marked **Used** or **OK to use**.
Anything under "Could not verify" stays out of the copy.

Note on access: fentontownship.org, michigan.gov (DNR), ecode360 and Municode sit behind a
bot check that blocked automated reads. For the township I read Internet Archive captures of
the township's own pages (capture URL and date given). For michigan.gov I read the pages with
a plain browser request (the same public page, not a copy).

## 1. City and township

- **F1. The city of Fenton was formed from land that was part of Fenton Township.** "The cities
  of Fenton and Linden were formed from areas formerly within the township."
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fenton_Township,_Michigan  **OK to use**
- **F2. Population (2020 census):** city of Fenton 12,050; Fenton Township 16,843.
  Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fenton,_Michigan and
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fenton_Township,_Michigan  (background only)
- **F3. Fenton Township is 27.5 sq mi, of which 3.7 sq mi (13.29%) is water.**
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fenton_Township,_Michigan  **Used** ("about 13 percent water")
- **F4. The city lies mostly in Genesee County, with small portions in Oakland and Livingston
  counties.** Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fenton,_Michigan  (background only)
- **F5. Lake Fenton CDP** (named for the lake) is part of the township and includes Lakeside and

## 2. Lakes (which are in or next to Fenton)

- **F6. Lakes in Fenton Township, per the Michigan DNR's local watercraft rules for Genesee
  County.** Each rule names the lake and "Fenton township": Barnum Lake, Crooked Lake,
  Dollar (Hibbard) Lake, **Lake Fenton** (rule covers "Fenton township and the city of Fenton"),
  **Lake Ponemah** (with a channel to a connected lake), Marl Lake (partly in Livingston County),
  McCully Lake, **Pine Lake**, and **Silver Lake** ("township of Fenton", section 33).
  Source: https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/managing-resources/laws/controls/localcontrols/genesee/watercraft
  **Used** (Lake Fenton, Lake Ponemah, Silver Lake, Pine Lake)
- **F7. Lake Fenton: 845 acres, max depth 92 ft, in Fenton Charter Township, formerly called
  Long Lake.** Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Fenton_(Michigan)  **Used** (845 acres)
- **F8. The township's own 2023 lake treatment page:** "Lake Ponemah, Tupper Lake, and Squaw Lake
  (Aanikegamaa Lake) are being treated as one body of water." Lake Fenton treatments were scheduled
  for the weeks of May 22 and June 2 (weed and algae) and July 17 and August 14 (starry stonewort),
  "subject to weather conditions". The township menu links a "Lake Treatment Schedule" page each year.
  Source (Internet Archive capture of the township page, 2023-05-25):
  https://web.archive.org/web/20230525205000/https://www.fentontownship.org/community-dept/page/lake-treatment-schedule-2023
  Live URL: https://www.fentontownship.org/community-dept/page/lake-treatment-schedule-2023
  **Used** (lakes are treated for weeds and algae in late spring and summer; the township posts a
  schedule). Copy does not name the third lake (its name is in transition).
- **F9. EGLE posting rule for lake treatments.** EGLE's aquatic nuisance control general permit:
  notice "must be posted prior to each chemical application ... and shall remain posted for a
  minimum of 24 hours or until all water use restrictions have expired, whichever is later," and
  "the brand name and water use restrictions listed on the federal pesticide label for the specific
  product used shall be included on the posting sign."
  Source: https://www.michigan.gov/egle/-/media/Project/Websites/egle/Documents/Programs/WRD/ANC/general-permit-template.pdf
  (linked from https://www.michigan.gov/egle/about/organization/water-resources/aquatic-nuisance-control)
  **Used**
- **F10. What the posting sign looks like.** A real EGLE-format posting (a different lake, used here
  only to confirm the sign's columns) has a column headed "Do Not Use this water for ornamentals or
  turf irrigation until" with a date per product. In that example the turf date was three days
  after treatment. Source: https://www.meridian.mi.us/media/al0pc2r0/may-19-2025-lake-lansing-treatment-notice-ma.pdf
  **Used** (the sign has a turf and ornamental irrigation date). The number of days is NOT used,
  because it varies by product.
- **F11. Lake Ponemah levels move with the season.** "The water levels on Lakes Ponemah, Squaw and
  Tupper are lowered every fall and then brought back up every spring." "The water level is
  controlled by the Linden Mill Pond dam." (WHMI, March 31, 2021)
  Source: https://whmi.com/news/article/39039  **Used** (Lake Ponemah drawn down in fall, raised in spring)
- **F12. Public DNR access sites on Lake Fenton and Lake Ponemah** are listed on the township's
  parks menu. Source (capture 2026-04-09):
  https://web.archive.org/web/20260409194529/https://www.fentontownship.org/community-dept/page/arsenic-well-water
  (background only)

## 3. Where the water comes from

### City of Fenton (city water)
- **F13. The city's water comes from four groundwater wells, each over 74 feet deep.**
  Source: City of Fenton Annual Water Quality Report for 2025, https://www.cityoffenton.org/Archive.aspx?ADID=318
  (listed at https://www.cityoffenton.org/Archive.aspx?AMID=51)  **Used**
- **F14. The city water plant softens the water.** "There are 4 groundwater wells with the capability
  of pumping 4.5 MGD for treatment." "The process of removing arsenic and other metals, such as
  calcium and magnesium (Hardness), is referred to as softening." The page does not mention iron removal.
  Source: https://www.cityoffenton.org/234/Water-Plant  **Used** ("four wells, treated at the city plant")
- **F15. City meters are read quarterly.** "Meters are read in the months of March, June, September
  and December, with bills being mailed out in April, July, October and January." "Water and sewer
  consumption charges are based on water meter readings."
  Source: https://www.cityoffenton.org/232/Utility-Billing-Meter-Reading  **Used**
- **F16. "Summer months are typically the highest for water consumption."** (2025 water quality report, p. 4)
  Source: https://www.cityoffenton.org/Archive.aspx?ADID=318  **Used** (repair FAQ)
- **F17. City water main flushing.** The distribution system is "divided into 4 quadrants and water
  mains are flushed on a rotation basis, typically 2 quadrants in the spring, and 2 quadrants in the
  fall." Notice is published in local newspapers and on the city website first. "Flushing operations
  are typically performed at night." Afterward "water may appear rust-colored because iron and other
  mineral deposits in the water main are stirred". The city suggests running the cold taps to clear it.
  Source: https://www.cityoffenton.org/233/Water-Main-Flushing-Program  **Used**
- **F18. City cross connection program.** PVBs are listed among testable backflow devices; "Residential
  users connected to the City of Fenton water system were brought into the cross connection program"
  in 2021. Source: https://www.cityoffenton.org/222/Cross-Connection-Program
  **NOT used**: it is about backflow testing, which Misty's has not confirmed they do. See questions.

### Fenton Township (wells, and new county water in the south)
- **F19. Private wells exist in the township.** The township's page on arsenic: elevated arsenic "have
  been found in the groundwater in some wells throughout Michigan, including Fenton Township," and
  "There are no regulatory requirements regarding arsenic levels in private wells." The township office
  hands out EGLE well water sample bottles ("EGLE Well Water Testing Kit" service; "Water sample bottles
  and the necessary forms are available at the Fenton Township office and the Genesee County Health
  Department").
  Source (capture 2026-04-09): https://web.archive.org/web/20260409194529/https://www.fentontownship.org/community-dept/page/arsenic-well-water
  Live URL: https://www.fentontownship.org/community-dept/page/arsenic-well-water
  **Used** only as: many township homes are on private wells, and the township office has well test
  bottles. Arsenic itself is NOT used (a health topic, not a sprinkler topic).
- **F20. New county water main in the southern township.** The Southern Lakes Water Extension "is being
  led by the Genesee County Drain Commissioner's Office as part of the Genesee County Water System
  (GCWS). This extension is bring municipal water to the southern portion of Genesee County. The water
  source for the GCWS is Lake Huron." Phase I went live December 2023 (Fenton Road and Thompson Road
  area); Phase II runs Torrey, Lahring and Jennings roads; Phase III runs Lahring to Ripley Road toward
  Linden. Connection is optional: "those that would like to connect" apply at the township office.
  Source (capture 2024-05-24): https://web.archive.org/web/20240524181358/http://www.fentontownship.org/planning/page/southern-lakes-water-extension
  **Used**
- **F21. Status in 2025, and sewer billing.** July 21, 2025 update: residents along Phases II and III
  were told "the system is live and they are now able to connect". For homes on township sewer,
  connecting to water changes the sewer bill: "Instead of the flat rate ... your sewer bill will be
  calculated" with a ready-to-serve charge plus a consumption charge per cubic foot of water used.
  Further phases (IV, V) were being planned in 2025.
  Source (capture 2026-03-21): https://web.archive.org/web/20260321160632/https://www.fentontownship.org/planning/page/southern-lakes-and-fenton-road-water-extension-projects
  **Used** (no fee amounts used anywhere in copy)

## 4. Iron in local well water

- **F22. USGS study of Genesee County groundwater:** "The water from the drift aquifer is hard or very
  hard and commonly contains objectionable amounts of iron." The report "describes the water resources
  of Genesee County". USGS Water-Supply Paper 1499-E, published 1964.
  Source: https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/wsp1499E  **Used** (as "a USGS study of the county's
  groundwater"; it is an older study, so copy says "commonly" and does not quote numbers)
- **F23. MDHHS "Iron in Drinking Water" fact sheet:** "Some areas in Michigan have naturally higher
  levels of iron in groundwater." Water with more than 0.3 mg/L of iron can cause "Staining of toilets,
  sinks, laundry and tubs." For private wells, "water testing is your responsibility"; ask for a test
  called "partial chemistry", which includes iron.
  Source: https://www.michigan.gov/mdhhs/-/media/Project/Websites/mdhhs/Safety-and-Injury-Prevention/Environmental-Health/Care-for-MiWell/Documents/Iron-in-Drinking-Water-FINAL-11-8-23.pdf
  (linked from https://www.michigan.gov/mdhhs/safety-injury-prev/environmental-health/topics/care-for-mi-drinking-water/contamination)
  **Used**
- Not found: any current, Fenton-specific iron measurements (EGLE, Genesee County Health Department or
  MSU Extension). Copy does not claim a Fenton iron level.

## 5. Soils

- **F24. Soil map units in a box drawn over Fenton Township and the city** (lon -83.805 to -83.687,
  lat 42.755 to 42.872). Queried 2026-11-10 from USDA NRCS Soil Data Access, the database behind Web
  Soil Survey (https://sdmdataaccess.sc.egov.usda.gov, tabular REST service). Share of the box by area:
  - loams and silt loams about 53% (largest: Conover loam 0 to 4% slopes about 14%; Wawasee loam
    2 to 6% about 13%; also Miami loam, Brookston loam, Del Rey silt loam)
  - loamy sands and sands about 14% (Boyer loamy sand, Boyer-Oshtemo loamy sands, Oakville fine sand)
  - sandy loams about 8% (Fox sandy loam, Gilford sandy loam, Wasepi sandy loam)
  - mucks about 12% (Carlisle and Linwood mucks, Edwards muck, Houghton and Adrian mucks, Rifle muck)
  - open water about 10%
  The query: `SELECT mu.musym, mu.muname, SUM(P.mupolygongeo.STIntersection(@aoi).STArea()) FROM
  mupolygon P JOIN mapunit mu ON mu.mukey = P.mukey WHERE P.mupolygongeo.STIntersects(@aoi) = 1
  GROUP BY mu.musym, mu.muname` with @aoi the box above. Anyone can check the same area in Web Soil
  Survey: https://websoilsurvey.nrcs.usda.gov/app/
  **Used** as "mostly loam, with sandy pockets and muck in the low spots". The box is approximate
  (it also clips a little of the neighbors), so copy gives no percentages.
- **F25. Conover series (the largest unit):** "somewhat poorly drained"; "Depth to the top of a perched
  seasonal high water table ranges from 15 to 46 cm below the surface at some time from September to
  May in normal years" (about 6 to 18 inches).
  Source: https://soilseries.sc.egov.usda.gov/OSD_Docs/C/CONOVER.html  **Used**
- **F26. Wawasee series:** "well drained", formed in till on moraines; permeability moderate.
  Source: https://soilseries.sc.egov.usda.gov/OSD_Docs/W/WAWASEE.html  **Used** (loams that drain well)
- **F27. Boyer series:** "very deep, well drained soils formed in sandy and loamy drift underlain by sand
  or gravelly sand outwash at depths of 51 to 102 cm (20 to 40 inches)"; saturated hydraulic conductivity
  high. Source: https://soilseries.sc.egov.usda.gov/OSD_Docs/B/BOYER.html  **Used** (sandy spots drain fast)
- **F28. Carlisle series:** "very poorly drained soils formed in woody and herbaceous organic materials in
  depressions"; the seasonal water table runs from 2 feet above the surface to 1 foot below "from
  September to June in normal years". Source: https://soilseries.sc.egov.usda.gov/OSD_Docs/C/CARLISLE.html
  **Used** (muck in low spots stays wet)

## 8. Digging rules (for installation)

  "If the contractor is doing the work, then it is the contractor's responsibility to contact MISS DIG 811."
