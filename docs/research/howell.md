# Howell, MI: local research for irrigation copy

Researched 2026-11-10. Covers the city of Howell and Howell Township (Livingston County),
plus the lakes nearby that carry Howell mailing addresses. Every fact below was read on the
page linked next to it. Anything I could not confirm on a page I read is in the
"Could not verify" section at the end and is NOT used in the copy.

Distance: Howell is about 30 minutes from Fenton and the farthest town Misty's serves
(project data, `src/data/cities.ts`, and the brief). No other logistics facts are claimed.

---

## 1. Where Howell is, and the city vs the township

- Howell is "the largest city in and the county seat of Livingston County". 2020 population
  10,068. Area 4.95 sq mi, of which 0.20 sq mi is water.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howell,_Michigan
- Howell Township "is in central Livingston County, on the north and west sides of the city of
  Howell". It is administered separately from the city. 32.0 sq mi, only 0.70% water.
  2020 population 7,893.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howell_Township,_Livingston_County,_Michigan
- Genoa Township is "bordered to the northwest by the city of Howell" (so Genoa, and Lake
  Chemung in it, lies to the southeast/east of the city).
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genoa_Township,_Michigan

## 2. Water supply

### City of Howell
- "Currently, water is supplied by five wells located in the city."
- The plant "utilizes a Claricone and rapid sand filters to soften and filter the well water."
  Page tagline: "Softened Groundwater". The current plant went online in 1992.
- History: "Before the completion of the current treatment facility in 1992," "City water was
  treated to remove hardness and iron by utilizing six zeolite ion exchange filters."
  (Local evidence that the groundwater under Howell carries iron.)
  Source: https://www.cityofhowell.org/city_departments/water_treatment/index.php
- Irrigation meters: the city takes Meter Request Applications from "Properties requesting a
  meter for lawn irrigation purposes" through the Department of Public Works; payment is due
  before installation is scheduled; scheduling through Utility Billing (517-546-3500).
- Right of way: any work in the city right of way needs a Right of Way Permit Application from
  the abutting property owner. The page lists "Lawn irrigation installations" among the examples
  of right of way work. The right of way is typically 66 feet wide, sidewalk to sidewalk; where
  there is no sidewalk, about 33 feet from the center of the road; some streets are only 50 feet.
  Source: https://www.cityofhowell.org/city_departments/forms_permits/index.php
- The city runs a Cross Connection Program (coordinator 517-546-7510).
  Source: https://www.cityofhowell.org/city_departments/department_public_works/related_departments/water_2/cross_connection_program.php
  NOT used in copy: it concerns backflow testing, which Misty's has not confirmed they do.

### Howell Township
- "public water is supplied through the Marion, Howell, Oceola, Genoa Water Authority (MHOG)."
- "Rural areas of the Township have fewer infrastructure demands, for example public utilities
  are not needed in these areas because buildings are situated on larger lots that can be served
  by well and septic systems."
  Source (Howell Township Master Plan, originally adopted Sept 25, 2016, posted as "Master Plan,
  November 2023"): https://howelltownshipmi.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/howell-township-master-plan-november-2016.pdf
  (pp. 12, 32)
- Utilities page: "It is highly encouraged to install an irrigation meter if you are watering
  lawns, landscaping, or filling your pool." "The irrigation meter exclusively measures water used
  for outdoor watering and irrigation." Outdoor water on that meter is billed for water only, not
  sewer. Contact the Zoning Administrator for pricing and process.
  Source: https://howelltownshipmi.org/utilities/
- Irrigation meter brochure (scanned image, read visually): benefits are "Accurate measurement of
  outdoor water use", "Reduced wastewater bills because wastewater charges only apply to indoor
  usage and not to the irrigation meter usage", and "Separate shutoff for the irrigation system".
  "dedicated irrigation meters are a great benefit to our customers and are encouraged."
  Source: https://howelltownshipmi.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Irrigation-Meter-Brochure.pdf
- "Does Your Water Bill Seem High?" (township utilities guideline):
  - Leak adjustments are handled under the MHOG Water and GO Sewer & Water Authorities guideline.
  - "Adjustments on water/sewer bills will not be made on the following: ... Irrigation systems
    and/or watering of lawns."
  - Its checklist asks: "Do you have an automatic sprinkler system with a broken head?" and
    "Have you put in a new lawn, sprinkler system or pool recently?"
  - "Outdoor watering can double or triple your water bill. Consider installing a second meter
    for outside watering, as this usage is only charged the water rate with no additional charge
    for sewer use."
  - "Water softeners are not necessary for customers with MHOG water."
  Source: https://howelltownshipmi.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Does-Your-Water-Bill-Seem-High.pdf

### Watering schedule or restriction
- None found. The City of Howell water treatment, DPW, water and permits pages and the Howell
  Township utilities page and documents list say nothing about an odd/even schedule or a ban.
  Web searches for a Howell or MHOG lawn watering schedule returned only other cities.
  Copy says nothing about a watering schedule.

## 3. Iron in well water

- Local: the city's own well water was "treated to remove hardness and iron" before 1992 (see
  above, cityofhowell.org water treatment page). This is the only official, Howell-specific iron
  statement I found.
- Livingston County, trade source only: a community post by a water treatment specialist at
  Advanced Water Treatment Inc. says "the iron from wells in Livingston County alone can be from
  0ppm (parts per million) to over 15ppm."
  Source: https://patch.com/michigan/brighton/the-challenges-and-rewards-of-treating-well-water
  NOT used as a number in copy (a vendor's post, not a public agency).
- Statewide context: USGS Open-File Report 87-0732, "Michigan Ground-Water Quality": "44 percent
  of the iron concentrations" in natural ground water samples equaled or exceeded the standard.
  Source: https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/1987/0732/report.pdf  (statewide; not used in copy)
- Livingston County Health Department residential wells page: new wells must be tested for
  bacteria, nitrates and arsenic; existing well owners are told to test "regularly". No iron
  guidance on the page.
  Source: https://milivcounty.gov/environmental-health/wells/residential/

Copy rule used: say the groundwater under Howell carries iron (city plant history), that a
private well gets no treatment unless the owner adds it, and point to Rid-O-Rust. No iron
numbers for Howell wells.

## 4. Lakes

### Thompson Lake (inside the city)
- Livingston County Drain Commission, Lakes and Dams: Thompson Lake, "City of Howell", 268.06
  acres, tributary area 7,808 acres. Legal level: summer 904.5; winter 901.5 in even years, 904.5
  in odd years. Court order 10/10/1984, modified 1989 and 1999. Thompson Lake Improvement Board.
  2026 notice: "Proposed start for lowering to winter lake level is October 9th, 2026."
  Source: https://milivcounty.gov/drain/lakes/
- Thompson Lake Improvement Board page: "The Court Order requires the lake to be lowered three
  feet every other year." "The winter drawdown is scheduled to begin on Friday, October 9th, 2026."
  "This date is tentative and highly dependent on weather." "The Thompson Lake Improvement Board
  currently oversees weed management on the lake." Hearings in May 2026 to continue the weed
  control project through 2028.
  Source: https://milivcounty.gov/drain/lakes/thompson/
- City master plan: "Three water resources lie within the city's boundaries, including Thompson
  Lake." Also lists "pesticides and fertilizers from lawns" among stormwater harms to lakes.
  Source: https://cms4files.revize.com/howellmi/Howell_MasterPlan_2023-2028%209-13-23.pdf (p. 66)
- Lakefront homes: lakeplace.com Howell listings (as of Nov 10, 2026) include a Thompson Shore
  Drive home described as "lakefront living" on Thompson Lake.
  Source: https://www.lakeplace.com/forsale/mi/howell/

### Lake Chemung (Genoa Township, Howell addresses)

## 5. Soils

- City master plan (Natural Features): "The glacially deposited clay, sand, and gravel mixture
  of Howell soils make it challenging to distinguish soil types." "Howell's topography is
  essentially flat at about 900 feet above sea level" and varies "only ... about 40 feet".
  Source: https://cms4files.revize.com/howellmi/Howell_MasterPlan_2023-2028%209-13-23.pdf (p. 66)
- USDA NRCS soil survey data (the database behind Web Soil Survey), Livingston County survey
  area MI093, version dated 8/27/2026. Queried through Soil Data Access
  (https://sdmdataaccess.sc.egov.usda.gov/), area-weighted share of map units:
  - Box around the city and east side (lon -83.99 to -83.86, lat 42.58 to 42.70): Wawasee loam
    (all slopes) 31.7%, Conover loam 8.1%, Carlisle muck 7.8%, Miami loam (steep) 5.7%,
    Spinks-Oakville loamy sands 4.8%, Boyer-Oshtemo loamy sands 4.1%, Brookston loam 2.7%,
    Owosso-Miami sandy loams 2.8%, Houghton muck 1.7%, Linwood muck 1.3%, water 1.7%.
    Grouped (top 25 units): loams and sandy loams about 59%, loamy sands about 10%, muck about 11%.
  - Box over Howell Township (lon -84.05 to -83.90, lat 42.59 to 42.70): Wawasee loam 20.7%,
    Conover loam 11%, Carlisle muck 8.8%, Owosso-Miami sandy loams 5.6%, Miami-Conover loams
    4.6%, Spinks-Oakville loamy sands 6.1%, Brookston loam 3.8%, Linwood muck 3.5%.
  - Component data: Wawasee and Miami are well drained loams; Conover loam is somewhat poorly
    drained; Brookston loam is poorly drained; Carlisle muck is very poorly drained;
    Spinks-Oakville and Boyer-Oshtemo are well drained loamy sands with much faster surface
    permeability (Ksat 92 um/s vs 1 to 9 um/s for the loams).
  Web Soil Survey: https://websoilsurvey.nrcs.usda.gov/
- Copy rule used: "mostly loam over glacial deposits, with sandy rises and muck in the low,
  wet ground." No percentages in copy.

## 6. Frost dates

- NOAA NCEI U.S. Climate Normals 1991 to 2020, station HOWELL WWTP (USC00203947, 42.5939 N,
  83.9325 W). 50 percent probability dates:
  - Last spring 32 F: May 4. Last spring 28 F: April 24. Last spring 36 F: May 16.
  - First fall 32 F: October 7. First fall 28 F: October 20. First fall 36 F: September 29.
  - Completeness flag "R" (Representative: meets WMO standards for 10 or more years); 11 years
    of data used for these values.
  Source: https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/data/normals-annualseasonal/1991-2020/access/USC00203947.csv
  Definitions and flags: https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/data/normals-annualseasonal/1991-2020/doc/Readme_By-Variable_By-Station_Normals_Files.txt
- Copy rule used: "around May 4" and "around October 7" for 32 F; "around April 24" and
  "around October 20" for a 28 F hard freeze; always framed as a typical year, not a promise.

## 7. Housing character

Page numbers below are PDF page numbers.

### City of Howell (Master Plan 2023 to 2028)
- "Fifty-seven percent (57%) of housing units in Howell were built before 1980, and nearly 87%
  of housing units were built before the 21st century." (p. 26)
- Core neighborhoods include the oldest (Piety Hill) and newest (Town Commons); grid streets;
  "lot sizes, widths, and building setbacks are small, with many of the existing homes and lots
  not meeting current zoning ordinance requirements." (p. 38)
- Suburban neighborhoods: "subdivisions" such as "the Heather Heath neighborhood on the west side
  of town or Rolling Oak on the north side of M-59"; larger lots; wider, winding streets, often
  cul-de-sacs. (pp. 38 to 39)
- "Much of the city's neighborhoods include mature trees in yards and along roadways." SEMCOG
  estimated 36.7% tree canopy in 2022. (p. 66)
- Howell Downtown Historic District listed on the National Register in 1987. (p. 12)
  Source: https://cms4files.revize.com/howellmi/Howell_MasterPlan_2023-2028%209-13-23.pdf

### Howell Township (Master Plan)
- "Most residents of Howell Township live in single-family homes in suburban neighborhoods or in
  large lots located throughout the Township with direct access to county roads. Much of the
  housing development is north of M-59 and Grand River." (p. 19)
- Suburban scale and medium density residential development surrounds the border with the city;
  otherwise "Farmland, open space, and large lot or estate residential parcels set in a pastoral
  country side with rolling terrain ... comprise the majority of the Township. Roughly 62% of the
  land in the Township is zoned Agricultural Residential. More than half of that land, or 35% of
  the Township, is active farm land." (p. 49)
  Source: https://howelltownshipmi.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/howell-township-master-plan-november-2016.pdf
