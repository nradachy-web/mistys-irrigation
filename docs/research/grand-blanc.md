# Grand Blanc, MI: irrigation research

Researched 2026-11-10. Every fact below was read on the page or document at the URL given.
"Grand Blanc" means two governments: the **City of Grand Blanc** (about four square miles in
the middle) and **Grand Blanc Charter Township** around it. Their water systems are different.

Never mention Flint in the copy. Several sources below name it; none of that is used.

## Water: who supplies what

### City of Grand Blanc: its own wells
- "The City of Grand Blanc has its own groundwater wells and treatment plants." Water is
  "filtered in a reverse-osmosis system before it is sent to homes and businesses", and the
  reverse osmosis plant "has the added benefit of softening the water."
  Source: https://www.cityofgrandblancmi.gov/our_residents/city_departments/public_works/water___sewer.php
  (also https://www.cityofgrandblancmi.gov/our_residents/city_departments/public_works/)
- 2025 Water Quality Report: "Your water came from 3 groundwater wells, each over 300 feet deep."
  All three source wells rated "low" susceptibility. It also tells city residents a mailing from
  the Genesee County Drain Commission "does not apply to you", which confirms city and township
  water are separate systems.
  Source: https://www.cityofgrandblancmi.gov/2025%20CCR.pdf?t=202602020849060
- Neither city page lists iron, a lawn watering schedule, an odd/even rule or sprinkler meters.

### Grand Blanc Township: Lake Huron water through the county, plus private wells
- Township water "ultimately comes from the Genesee County Drain Commission water treatment
  plant and Karegnondi Water Authority intake." The township public water supply "serves more
  than 18,000 residents."
  Source: https://grandblanctwpmi.gov/m/faq?cat=21
- Source changed in November 2017 from the Great Lakes Water Authority to the Genesee
  County/Karegnondi system; both draw from Lake Huron.
  Source: https://grandblanctwpmi.gov/199/Drinking-Water
  (The 2021 Master Plan says the switch was 2018. Copy gives no year, and never "since 2017".)
- Master Plan (Sept 2021): the township system serves about 21,000 people (8,060 customers);
  water is bought from the Genesee County Drain Commissioner's Division of Water and Waste
  Services; "The remainder of Grand Blanc Township residents rely on private wells."
  Source: https://www.grandblanctwpmi.gov/DocumentCenter/View/1056
  (The FAQ says 18,000+ residents, the plan says about 21,000 people. Copy uses neither number.)

### Private wells in the township
- "Many areas of the Township are not served by Township water and must drill water wells."
  Wells are drilled by private companies and permitted by the Genesee County Health Department.
- Existing homes on a well do not have to connect. "Newly built homes that have public water
  available to them must connect to the public water supply and may not drill a well."
  Once connected, a home may not disconnect to drill a new well.
- **Irrigation wells:** "Township water customers may install an irrigation well for watering
  lawns and flowerbeds. However, it must not enter the home at any point and be 100% contained
  outside of the home. This will require an initial and annual inspection by a Township Official."
- **Old house well kept for irrigation:** "a private well that is no longer being used for
  domestic purposes ... can be used for irrigation. This is upon approval of the Director of
  Public Works or his/her designee." Removed from the home, contained outside, initial and annual
  inspection by a township official.
  Source for all four bullets: https://grandblanctwpmi.gov/m/faq?cat=21
- Master Plan: when a subdivision on private wells gets a road project, the township encourages
  it to bring in municipal water at the same time. The large-lot southwest corner is "not well
  served by municipal sewer and water."
  Source: https://www.grandblanctwpmi.gov/DocumentCenter/View/1056

### Lawn meters, billing and turn-offs (township)
- Water is billed "per 100 Cubic Feet (748 Gallons) of water used."
- "Lawn Meters are billed an RTS fee monthly, year-round whether utilized or not, based on size."
  (RTS is the ready to serve fee.)
  Source: https://grandblanctwpmi.gov/190/Current-Utility-Rates
- The 2026 fee schedule lists "Irrigation Meter Branch Fitting (Price Includes Meters)" in four
  sizes, and a "Water Turn On/Off (For Vacations, Winterization, and Non-Payment)" fee that
  "Covers Fall Turn Off and Spring Turn On."
  Source: https://www.grandblanctwpmi.gov/DocumentCenter/View/2481/2026-Fee-Schedule
  (No prices in the copy, ever.)

### Watering rules
- Township: Master Plan says township residents on public water "must comply with Genesee County
  standards. For example, the Genesee County Drain Commissioner regularly sends out Water
  Conservation Notices, typically during summer months when demand for water is higher. These
  notices restrict certain activities during a specified period such as lawn watering, filling
  swimming pools, or washing cars."
  Source: https://www.grandblanctwpmi.gov/DocumentCenter/View/1056
- **Not verified:** any standing odd/even schedule or watering hours for the township or the
  city. Searched both sites, the township DPW page (https://grandblanctwpmi.gov/dpw) and the
  county WWS site (https://www.gcdcwws.com/, /public-notice, /helpful-tips, /latest-news): none
  posts one. The city code (American Legal) and an ecode360 page that may or may not be the
  township's both blocked automated reading. Copy says only that summer conservation notices
  can limit lawn watering on township water, and never states an odd/even rule.

## Iron in well water
- USGS Water-Supply Paper 1499-E (1964), which covers all of Genesee County: "The water from the
  drift aquifer is hard or very hard and commonly contains objectionable amounts of iron."
  Iron in drift wells ranged "from about 0.1 to 2.2 ppm." Water from the deeper Saginaw
  formation also has iron, though generally less than the drift. The report's water quality
  table: "On exposure to air, iron in ground water oxidizes to reddish-brown sediment. More than
  about 0.3 ppm stains laundry and utensils reddish brown."
  Source: https://pubs.usgs.gov/wsp/1499e/report.pdf
  (Catalog page: https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/wsp1499E. The title names a city we do not
  mention; copy says "a U.S. Geological Survey study of Genesee County groundwater".)
- **Not verified:** a current EGLE, MSU Extension or Genesee County Health Department page on iron
  in Grand Blanc wells specifically. The michigan.gov MDHHS water page refused automated reading.
  The USGS study is old, so copy calls iron common in county well water, not in every well.

## Soils
- USDA NRCS Soil Data Access (the database behind Web Soil Survey), queried 2026-11-10 at
  https://sdmdataaccess.sc.egov.usda.gov/Tabular/post.rest on an 8 by 8 grid of 64 points across
  the city and township (latitude 42.870 to 42.975, longitude -83.695 to -83.560):
  - Conover loam (several slope phases): 22 of 64 points. Celina-Conover loams: 11. Celina
    loam: 3. So about half the points are Conover or Celina loam.
  - Also present: Urban land-Crosier complex (5, built-up ground), Wawasee loam (4), Brookston
    loam (4), Houghton and Adrian mucks (4), Boyer and Metea loamy sands (3), Lenawee silty clay
    loam, Miami loam and clay loam, Marlette loam, Gilford sandy loam.
  - Drainage class of the main component: somewhat poorly drained 22, moderately well drained
    14, well drained 12, poorly or very poorly drained 10.
- Conover series: "very deep, somewhat poorly drained soils that are moderately deep or deep to
  dense till", formed "in loamy till on moraines and till plains." Saturated hydraulic
  conductivity is "very low or low in the dense till." "Depth to the top of a perched seasonal
  high water table ranges from 15 to 46 cm below the surface at some time from September to May
  in normal years" (about 6 to 18 inches). Conover is "extensive, with over 400,000 acres mapped"
  in southern Michigan, southern Wisconsin and northern Indiana.
  Source: https://soilseries.sc.egov.usda.gov/OSD_Docs/C/CONOVER.html
- Celina series: "very deep, moderately well drained soils that are moderately deep to dense
  till", formed in up to 18 inches of loess over loamy till. "Permeability is moderately slow
  above the dense till, and very slow in the dense till."
  Source: https://soilseries.sc.egov.usda.gov/OSD_Docs/C/CELINA.html
- County level, USGS 1964: "Heavy clay and silt-loam soils with poor drainage predominate" in
  Genesee County. Source: https://pubs.usgs.gov/wsp/1499e/report.pdf

## Frost dates
- NOAA NCEI 1991 to 2020 Climate Normals, station GOODRICH, MI US (USC00203278), at 42.9164 N,
  83.5097 W. That is roughly six miles east of downtown Grand Blanc (straight line, my
  calculation from the station coordinates and the city center at about 42.93 N, 83.63 W).
  Median (50% probability) dates:
  - Last spring 32F freeze: **May 1**. Last spring 28F (hard) freeze: **April 22**.
  - First fall 32F freeze: **October 14**. First fall 28F (hard) freeze: **October 28**.
  - Median growing season above 32F: 164 days.
  Source: https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/services/data/v1?dataset=normals-annualseasonal-1991-2020&stations=USC00203278&format=json
  Element definitions: https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/pub/data/cdo/documentation/normals-annualseasonal-1991-2020_documentation.pdf
  (Only the 50% dates are used. The 10% and 90% wording in the documentation is ambiguous.)

## Housing and neighborhoods
All from the Grand Blanc Township Master Plan, September 2021:
https://www.grandblanctwpmi.gov/DocumentCenter/View/1056
- In 1930 "the four square miles in the center of the Township became the incorporated City of
  Grand Blanc."
- Most township housing units were built after 1970.
- Residential use is about 66% of township acreage, "principally in the form of single-family
  subdivisions." Lots are smaller and denser in the northern half; "Progressing further south in
  the Township, residential lots become roomier, with both spacious individual parcels and
  subdivisions with larger lots." The southwest corner is planned for "rural estate" lots of at
  least one acre.
- "there are 80 total neighborhoods in Grand Blanc Township", "Most of these neighborhoods
  consist of residential subdivisions, and there are many homeowners' associations", and
  residents "typically refer to the name of their subdivision when describing where they live."
- By census tract: the north is mostly 1970s housing; "most housing units directly west of the
  City of Grand Blanc ... were constructed between 1960 and 1969"; the southern half is newer,
  with 2000 to 2009 the decade with the most homes built in each of its three tracts.
  Table 3 shares for the top decade: 1960s west of the city 26%; 1970s in the two northern
  tracts 34% and 40%; 2000s in the three southern tracts 34% to 37%. These are pluralities,
  not majorities, so the copy says "busiest building decade", not "most homes".
- The southern half of the township has 2000s as its busiest decade; copy rounds the systems
  there to "around 20 years old" (2026 minus the 2000s).
