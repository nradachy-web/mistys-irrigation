# Holly, MI: local research for irrigation copy

Researched 2026-11-10. Holly means two places: the **Village of Holly** and **Holly Township**
(Oakland County), which surrounds the village. Every fact below was read on the page given.
Copy may only use facts marked **Use**. Anything marked **Not verified** stays out of the copy.

## Where the water comes from

1. **Village water is groundwater from four village wells.** "Your water comes from four (4) wells
   located on the West side of town. Wells are approximately 210 feet deep and are embedded into
   sandstone (Marshall Formation)." No iron result or iron treatment is listed in the report.
   Source: Village of Holly, 2025 Water Quality Report,
   https://hollyvillage.org/images/2025%20Water%20Quality%20Report%20for%20the%20Village%20of%20Holly%20CCR.pdf
   **Use.** (Do not say village water is high in iron: the report does not say so.)

2. **Village system hardware.** "four wells connected to miles of pipes", "several pressure booster
   stations", "three water towers with a capacity of 875,000 gallons"; weekly, monthly and annual
   sampling. Source: Village of Holly Public Works,
   https://hollyvillage.org/index.php/government/public-works **Use.**

3. **The township has no municipal water; most township homes are on private wells.**
   "Holly Township does not provide municipal water or sewer services." And: "water supply for
   Township residents is provided mostly by individual wells (as well as some who are connected to
   Village water and sewer)". Source: Holly Township Master Plan, adopted August 21, 2024, pages 18
   and 48. Original URL (now returns 404 after the township moved to hollytownship.gov):
   https://hollytownship.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/2024-Adopted-MAster-Plan-FINAL.pdf
   Read via the Internet Archive copy:
   https://web.archive.org/web/20251025030537/https://hollytownship.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/2024-Adopted-MAster-Plan-FINAL.pdf
   **Use.**

4. **Township groundwater concerns named in the plan are arsenic and chloride, not iron.** "areas
   with high contamination levels of arsenic and chloride in some areas of the Township." Same
   master plan, page 19. Not irrigation relevant; **not used** (health topic, not ours).

## Iron in well water

5. **Iron is common in Oakland County well water (county wide, not Holly specific).** USGS
   Water-Resources Investigations Report 00-4120, *Ground-Water Quality Atlas of Oakland County*
   (Aichele, 2000), done with the Oakland County Health Division: "Water from the 38 wells sampled
   by the USGS did not exceed the SMCL or MCL for sulfate, fluoride, or nitrite. However, water from
   26 wells exceeded the SMCL for iron". The iron SMCL in the report's table is 0.3 mg/L. On effects:
   iron and manganese "leave deposits in pipes and on fixtures, impart taste to beverages, and can
   discolor laundry." Also: "The Marshall Sandstone is a productive bedrock aquifer for the
   northwestern townships of Holly, Groveland, Brandon, and Rose. Even in this area, the vast
   majority of wells are completed in the glacial sediments."
   Source: https://mi.water.usgs.gov/pubs/WRIR/WRIR00-4120/pdf/wholereport.pdf
   **Use**, worded as Oakland County (26 of 38 wells, 1998 sampling). The per-well iron table does
   not name townships, so never say "Holly wells are high in iron" as a measured fact.

6. **Oakland County Health Division on iron and irrigation wells.** Iron is listed with hardness,
   chloride, tannins, manganese, sulfates and hydrogen sulfide as "compounds that can affect the
   visual quality of the water, but most do not cause adverse health problems if they are
   monitored." County lab partial chemistry is "Nitrate, Nitrite, Chloride, Fluoride and Sulfate"
   (iron is not on that panel). "Testing for iron bacteria requires a specific test." An irrigation
   well is one "constructed for the purpose of supplying water for other than potable drinking
   purposes" (lawn watering is the example), and those permit applications go on a paper form.
   Source: https://www.oaklandcountymi.gov/community/health/public-health-services/environmental-health/well-and-water-services
   **Use** (no fee amounts in copy).

## Soil and ground

7. **Glacial, hilly ground with mixed soils.** "Holly Township is situated within one of two broad
   zones in the southeast Michigan area; the hilly zone. The largest surface geological form in Holly
   Township is the area of till plain, closely followed by the morainic form. The outwash plain
   accounts for the least area." Four general soil associations: Marlette-Capac-Houghton (loamy and
   mucky; the most prevalent), Oshtemo-Spinks-Houghton (sandy and mucky), Riddles-Marlette-Houghton
   (loamy and mucky), and a very small area of Fox-Oshtemo-Houghton. "The Village of Holly and the
   most intensively developed portions of the Township, lie within the Marlette-Capac-Houghton soil
   type and the area of outwash plain." Source: 2024 Master Plan, page 16 (URLs in item 3). **Use.**
   Not checked: parcel level USDA Web Soil Survey (it is an interactive map; the master plan's
   general soil mapping is the source used).

## Lakes

8. **More than 35 lakes.** "More than 35 lakes of varying size are scattered extensively throughout
   the Township, occupying 6% of total land area." The Shiawassee River crosses the southern part of
   the township; Swartz Creek is in the central north. The plan also says the lakes, rivers and
   wetlands are why few roads run straight through. Source: 2024 Master Plan, pages 15 and 18.
   **Use.**

9. **Bush Lake** (Michigan DNR, Status of the Fishery Resource Report 0440, 2025): "a 110-acre lake
   near Holly"; "The substrate in Bush Lake is dominated by sand in areas less than ten ft deep and
   organic muck at greater depths"; "aquatic vegetation is abundant in the littoral zone"; maximum
   depth 47 ft in the southwest basin, a shallower north basin past a sandy shoal; "Water flows into
   Bush Lake via a narrow canal under Grange Hall Road from Lake Mauna Loa. Water leaves the lake over
   Bush Lake Dam. The gravity dam is owned by the Village of Holly." About 50% of the shoreland is
   disturbed; "Several riparian owners have manicured turf grass to the water's edge. Native
   plantings along the shoreline and buffer strips with no-mow sections should be implemented."
   Source: https://www.dnr.state.mi.us/PUBLICATIONS/PDFS/DNRFishLibrary/StatusoftheFisheryResourceReports/0440_2025_Bush_Lake.pdf
   **Use.** This is the verified case of private lawns running to a Holly lake.

10. **Bush Lake shore and parks.** "A large portion of the area to the east of Bush Lake is
    built-out"; "Holly Township Beach Park and Lakeside Park are located at the southwest corner of
    Bush Lake". Table 29 lists a Village of Holly boat launch on Bush Lake. Source: 2024 Master Plan,
    pages 52 and the natural features section. **Use.**

11. **State park lakes.** Seven Lakes State Park: opened 1977, 1,444 acres, "The 230 acres of water
    that give the park its name"; Big Seven Lake 170 water acres and Dickinson Lake 44 (Table 29).
    Holly State Recreation Area: 7,817 acres shared with Groveland Township; Heron Lake 132 acres,
    Crotched 14, Holdridge 16 (Table 29). Source: 2024 Master Plan, pages 50 to 52. **Use** only as
    state parkland. Not verified: private homes on any of these lakes.

12. **Great Lakes Community.** "located at the southeastern portion of Holly Township near the
    Groveland Township border, was originally platted in the 1930's and has a number of historic
    lots"; a "slower pace 'up-north' lake front community. The variety of lot sizes, lakefront, and
    natural wooded and rural landscape define this geography". Development guideline: "Expansive
    landscaped lots and broadly sodded fronts are discouraged in this district." Source: 2024 Master
    Plan, page 97. **Use** (it is a planning guideline, not an ordinance; say so).

13. **Stiff's Mill Pond, Village of Holly.** EGLE's Dam Safety Unit ordered an emergency drawdown in
    July 2025; the village opposed it (WHMI, July 21, 2025: https://www.whmi.com/news/article/48315).
    Fox 2 (July 21, 2025): the target was "six inches per day until Mill Pond is down four feet", and
    "the forty or so homeowners with waterfront property are watching the water drain away."
    Source: https://www.fox2detroit.com/news/village-holly-injunction-would-halt-mill-pond-drainage-denied
    **Use** only as a dated fact (July 2025). **Not verified:** the pond's level today, whether
    repairs were made, or whether any Mill Pond homes water from the pond.

14. **Lake Mauna Loa** is in Holly Township (https://www.lakehomes.com/michigan/lake-mauna-loa, a
    real estate page). Its acreage conflicts between sources (11 vs 20). **Use** only as the lake
    that feeds Bush Lake (DNR, item 9).

15. **Lake Shannon is not in Holly.** Listings place it in Tyrone Township, Livingston County, with
    Fenton mailing addresses (for example https://livinginmichigan.com/properties/listing/Realcomp/20261046007/Tyrone-Township/7291-Parkwood-Drive).
    **Not used** for Holly.

## Housing and land

16. **Land use (2023):** single family 26%, agricultural 21%, recreation and conservation 19%, vacant
    16%, water 7%. "Half of the existing housing stock in Holly Township was constructed before
    1990. The greatest percentage of new housing stock, 31%, was built between 2000 and 2010." Goal
    language: "low density rural residential on acreage" and "historic single family residential
    neighborhoods in the Village". "Located in northwest Oakland County it truly feels like 'up
    north'." Source: 2024 Master Plan, pages 5, 31, 59 and 68. **Use.**

17. **Village size.** 2020 census: 5,997 people, 2,683 housing units; Holly Township 12,006 people.
    Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holly,_Michigan and
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holly_Township,_Michigan **Background only.**

18. **Getting there from Fenton.** "Grange Hall Road is the major east-west route linking Holly
    Township and Village with Holly State Recreation Area to the east and the City of Fenton to the
    west." The Shiawassee canoe trail runs "starting in Waterworks Park and ending at Fenton Mill
    Pond", "seven river miles". Source: 2024 Master Plan, pages 15 and 115. **Use.** Drive time
    ("About 15 minutes") comes from src/data/cities.ts.
