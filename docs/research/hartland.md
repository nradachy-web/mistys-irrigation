# Hartland, MI: local research for irrigation copy

Researched 2026-11-10. Every fact below was read on the page whose URL follows it.
Anything I could not confirm on a page I actually read is in "Not verified" at the
bottom and is kept out of the copy.

Note: the township's documents are about Hartland **Township** (Livingston County).
"Hartland" the settlement is a small unincorporated village inside it (777 people in
2020, per the Wikipedia article below). The copy says "Hartland" and "Hartland Township".

## Place and roads

- Hartland Township had **15,256 residents at the 2020 census** (14,663 in 2010). Total area
  37.3 sq mi, of which 1.4 sq mi is water. **US-23 crosses the township as a four lane freeway**
  and **M-59 crosses it south of Hartland village**. Unincorporated communities: Hallers
  Corners, Hartland, Parshallville. Drainage named: North Ore Creek, South Ore Creek.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartland_Township,_Michigan
- The township is "approximately 10 miles north of the City of Brighton and approximately
  10 miles east of the City of Howell." M-59 crosses east to west, US-23 north to south.
  Clyde Road crosses the whole township and "is a gravel road west of U.S. 23." "Livingston
  County has jurisdiction over 94 of the 106 miles of public roads in the Township and 37
  of those 94 miles are unpaved." (2020-2021 Comprehensive Plan Amendment, adopted May 18, 2021, p. 5)
  https://www.hartlandtwp.com/sites/default/files/fileattachments/planning/page/8630/2020-2021_comp_plan_amendment_05.18.2021.pdf
- Drive from Fenton: "About 20 minutes" (project data, `src/data/cities.ts`; not a web fact).

## Lakes

- The township's own zoning map (reflects zoning as of 05/12/25) labels these water bodies:
  **Long Lake, Handy Lake, Round Lake, Maxfield Lake, Dunham Lake, Bullard Lake, Silver Lake,
  Blaine Lake, Grubb Lake, Whalen Lake, Divine Lake, Wallace Lake, Lake Walden, Tyrone Lake,
  Parshallville Pond.** The 2020-2021 Future Land Use Map labels the same set.
  https://www.hartlandtwp.com/sites/default/files/fileattachments/planning/page/4747/hartland_zoning_map_06.13.2025.pdf
  https://www.hartlandtwp.com/sites/default/files/fileattachments/planning/page/4747/2020-2021_future_land_use_map_10.23.2025.pdf
  (So the four names in the brief are all verified: Long, Handy, Dunham, Bullard.)
- "The Township has **thirteen (13) significant water bodies** that are connected through an
  extensive watercourse network." (2021 amendment, p. 24) Same plan: "many units have been
  constructed on the numerous lakes in Hartland Township, and ... many have chosen to live in
  Hartland specifically for this particular amenity." (p. 23)
  https://www.hartlandtwp.com/sites/default/files/fileattachments/planning/page/8630/2020-2021_comp_plan_amendment_05.18.2021.pdf
- Which lakes have homes (2021 amendment, pp. 15 to 16, same URL as above):
  - "The areas adjacent to **Round, Handy and Maxfield Lakes**, Millpointe subdivision, and
    Cobblestone Reserve site condominiums" are Medium Urban Density Residential, which is meant
    "to reflect the existing densities and character of the identified areas": about two or
    three homes per acre, "Lot sizes are anticipated to be **8,000 to 20,000 square feet** per dwelling."
  - Medium Suburban Density Residential "has been assigned to the areas adjacent to **Tyrone Lake
    and Dunham Lake**"; half an acre to one acre per home.
  - Low Suburban Density (one to two acres per home) includes land "around the perimeter of
    **Long Lake**."
- Lakefront character (2004 Comprehensive Plan, pp. 27 and 32): "Located along the shoreline of
  the majority of the lakes, a large number of single family homes occupy the Township's lakefront
  access. Many of these homes were initially established as **cottages but have evolved into
  year-round residences**. The home sites are characterized by **smaller lots, homes oriented
  towards the lake and neighborhoods with mature trees and unpaved roads**. Recently, many of the
  older cottage style homes have been **expanded or torn down** to accommodate the trend for larger
  single family residences." Also: "not all of the lakes within the Township have seen development
  along their shores." The township "acquired a significant amount of park land with frontage on
  Bullard Lake."
  https://www.hartlandtwp.com/sites/default/files/fileattachments/planning/page/2521/comprehensive-plan.pdf
- **Long Lake** (listed under Hartland Township by the Livingston County Drain Commissioner):
  lake size 174.23 acres; **legal lake level 953.0**, established 5/26/1947; tributary area 2,816
  acres. The Drain Commissioner's Office held a public hearing on the special assessment roll and
  cost "for repairs to the **Long Lake Dam**" on Wednesday, December 17, 2025, at Hartland Township Hall.
  https://milivcounty.gov/drain/lakes/
- Lake Walden is the site of the Waldenwoods resort and the Majestic at Lake Walden golf course
  (2021 amendment, p. 5, URL above). Not useful for homeowner copy.

## Where the water comes from

- Township water system (2025 Water Quality Report): "Hartland Township's water treatment plant
  currently receives source water from **three active submersible wells**." Wells can produce
  2.594 million gallons per day. "The treatment plant uses an **iron/manganese removal system** to
  treat source water prior to the distribution process." About twenty four miles of mains, 4 to
  18 inch. "Hartland Township currently distributes water to **over 836 homes and businesses**."
  "Hartland Township municipal water is sourced from three groundwater wells with depths in excess
  of 200 feet." The system has 721 water service lines and no lead lines.
  https://www.hartlandtwp.com/sites/default/files/fileattachments/public_works/page/1701/hartland_township_2025_ccr_final.pdf
- "Hartland Township depends upon groundwater sources to supply drinking water to **select areas**
  of the Township." (Wellhead Protection page and its PDF)
  https://www.hartlandtwp.com/node/1671
  https://www.hartlandtwp.com/sites/default/files/fileattachments/public_works/page/1671/aboutwellheadprotection.pdf
- History (2004 Comprehensive Plan, pp. 54, 43 and 36): "During 2002, the Township completed the
  installation of a public water system." The plant and tower are north of M-59 and west of Old
  US-23. "At this time access to public water service is limited to the commercial development
  within close proximity of the M-59 and US-23 interchange. Limited residential areas currently have
  access to this new system. **Those residents who do not have access to public water utilize private
  wells** to supply water." Also: "Outside of these areas, private septic fields and wells were
  established for each residence or building." The plan speaks of "those parts of the Township where
  privately supplied well water will continue to be the primary source for drinking water into the
  foreseeable future."
  https://www.hartlandtwp.com/sites/default/files/fileattachments/planning/page/2521/comprehensive-plan.pdf
- Conclusion used in copy (inference from the facts above, stated as such): the township system
  serves a little over 830 homes and businesses in a township of about 15,000 people, so most
  homes in Hartland are on a private well.

## Iron in the water

- Township Water page: "The Township water hardness levels are approximately **464 mg/L**, with a
  **total iron of 0.5 mg/L**." "These numbers are often used to program homeowner's water softeners."
  https://www.hartlandtwp.com/node/1701
- The township's own plant runs an iron/manganese removal system on its wells (2025 report, above).
- EGLE fact sheet, Iron Bacteria in Wells (Rev. 08/2020): "Because **iron is common in Michigan
  groundwater**, it is not unusual to find iron bacteria in well water." Iron bacteria leave "a
  reddish-brown slime" and "Biofilm deposition can clog pump intakes, well screens, filters, and
  water pipes."
  https://www.michigan.gov/egle/-/media/Project/Websites/egle/Documents/Programs/DWEHD/Water-Well-Construction/iron-bacteria-in-wells.pdf
- EGLE fact sheet, Manganese in Drinking Water Wells (Rev. 9/2025): manganese "is commonly found
  with iron"; "iron can cause an **orange or brown stain** in sinks and in the laundry, manganese
  often results in a dense black stain." Recommends testing total iron, manganese, hardness,
  alkalinity and pH before buying treatment equipment.
  https://www.michigan.gov/egle/-/media/Project/Websites/egle/Documents/Programs/DWEHD/Water-Well-Construction/Fact-Sheet-Manganese-in-Wells.pdf

## Watering rules and the township water bill

- No standing odd/even or day-of-week lawn watering rule found on the township site.
- Temporary request, June 2024: Hartland Township Public Works asked "all residents connected to the
  Hartland Township water system" to "cease watering lawns and landscape areas until Monday, July 1st"
  because one well was out of service for maintenance; "there are no safety concerns regarding the
  water." DPW: (810) 632-7498. Township page has no date; WHMI dates it June 26, 2024.
  https://www.hartlandtwp.com/node/8510
  https://whmi.com/news/article/45764
- Water Billing page: "Meters are read at the end of each quarter and bills are mailed around the
  10th of the following month." "High metered usage is often caused by **irrigation systems** and
  leaking toilets or faucets." "For residents on municipal water and sewer, **separate irrigation
  meters may be purchased at the Township Hall** to segregate household usage from irrigation usage,
  which can result in a large savings."
  https://www.hartlandtwp.com/publicworks/page/water-billing

## Soils

Source: 2004 Comprehensive Plan, pp. 42 to 43, summarizing the Soil Survey of Livingston County.
https://www.hartlandtwp.com/sites/default/files/fileattachments/planning/page/2521/comprehensive-plan.pdf

- "Six soil associations are generally found in Hartland Township."
- Fox-Boyer-Oshtemo: steep or hilly moraines, hilltops and ridgetops; "well drained"; the three
  major soils "overlay **gravelly sand**."
- Spinks-Oakville-Boyer-Oshtemo: strongly sloping to hilly uplands; well drained; Boyer and Oshtemo
  "underlain by gravelly sand"; "low in fertility."
- Carlisle-Houghton-Gilford: nearly level soils on outwash plains, glacial drainageways and lake
  plains; "**very poorly drained, low-lying soils**."
- Miami-Hillsdale and Miami-Conover: moraines and till plains; Miami and Hillsdale well drained;
  Conover "somewhat poorly drained, lower lying."
- Oshtemo-Kalamazoo: "throughout the Township, generally in the north, northeast and south."
- "In general, the more poorly drained soils are located in floodplains along **Ore Creek** and in
  the western portion of the Township."
- USDA Web Soil Survey itself was not read (interactive tool); the township plan's summary of the
  county soil survey is the source used.

## Housing character

- 2004 Comprehensive Plan, p. 16: housing types include "large homes on relatively large lots within
  subdivisions or site condominiums, cottages and large homes along the waterfront, small homes on
  small lots within the settlement areas, and farm homes on large acreage parcels." p. 26 to 27:
  rural residential "is the largest type of single family development," homes "typically constructed
  on large lots on the county road frontage"; newer suburban areas mostly "lot sizes of an acre or
  less with cul-de-sac and curvilinear streets"; the Hartland settlement area is a grid, "Homes in
  these areas are older than most homes in the Township."
  https://www.hartlandtwp.com/sites/default/files/fileattachments/planning/page/2521/comprehensive-plan.pdf
- 2021 amendment, pp. 14 to 15: Estate Residential "encompasses **nearly half of the land** in the
  Township," at two or more acres per home; Rural Residential is three or more acres per home.
  Medium Suburban Density (half acre to one acre) includes "Hartland Estates, San Marino Estates,
  Meadow View Estates, Autumn Woods and other similar single-family developments."
  https://www.hartlandtwp.com/sites/default/files/fileattachments/planning/page/8630/2020-2021_comp_plan_amendment_05.18.2021.pdf

## Frost dates

- Almanac.com frost dates for ZIP 48353 (Hartland) use the station **MILFORD GM PROVING GROUND, MI**
  (971 ft): **last spring frost May 13**, **first fall frost Oct 5**, growing season 144 days. "Last
  spring and first fall frost dates use a **30% probability** threshold." Data: "1991-2020 Climate
  Normals from NOAA."
  https://www.almanac.com/gardening/frostdates/zipcode/48353
- Copy wording: "the nearest long term station, at the Milford proving ground, averages a last spring
  frost around May 13 and a first fall frost around October 5; about 3 years in 10 the spring frost
  comes later and the fall frost earlier."
